Launching

Your Kestrel waits on the pad with the clock holding at T-10. Nothing happens until you press Entertap START: that starts the 10-second countdown. The engines light at T-3 and the clamps let go at zero. In a hurry? During the countdown press Enter againtap SKIP to launch straight away. The sky turns from blue to black as you climb, and the engine flame spreads out wide once there's no air around it. Engines are a bit weaker down in the thick air and get stronger as the air thins.

Getting to orbit

Full throttle straight up. At about 2 km, start leaning left, gently: a full, heavy rocket turns slowly. Left is the way Luna travels round Terra, which makes the trip to Luna cheaper later. Leaning right works too: the mission guidance follows whichever way you lean, all the way to Luna. By 10 km you should be about 40° over, by 30 km about 70°. Watch Air push (max-Q): if the red MAX-Q warning flashes, throttle down a little or the rocket breaks apart. When the booster runs dry, press Spacetap STAGE (you'll see a puff of smoke as it falls away). Above about 45 km, drop the fairing (FFAIRING): it's dead weight up there. Dropping it in thick air can hit the rocket. Keep burning until Apoapsis (shown live, also above the dial) is above 80 km, cut the engine, warp to the apoapsis and burn sideways (green prograde marker) until your Periapsis is above 70 km too. You're in orbit!

Getting to Luna

From orbit, open the map (MMap button). Wait until Luna is a bit more than a quarter turn ahead of you, then burn along your direction of travel (the green marker) until your orbit stretches out to Luna. A purple "Luna encounter" box appears when you'll arrive. Near Luna, burn backwards (red marker) to slow down, then land on the upper stage's legs: under 8 m/s, standing upright. There's no air on Luna, so no parachute there.

Coming home to Terra

From orbit, point backwards (red marker) and burn until your Periapsis is about 25–35 km. Then drop the upper stage so only the capsule is left. Keep the capsule's dark heat shield (its bottom) facing the way you're moving; the capsule tends to swing that way by itself in the air. It glows as it hits the air. Come in too steep or too fast and the Heat reading climbs past 100% and the capsule burns up. Once you've slowed below 250 m/s, open the parachute (PCHUTE); faster than that and it tears. Touch down under 9 m/s on land or 14 m/s in the ocean. Launch, orbit and come home safely to earn Round Trip.

Missions and guidance (optional)

When your Kestrel is on the pad, a Pick a mission panel shows up. You can ignore it and just fly (Free flight), or pick one: Low orbit (LEO, 100 km), Medium orbit (MEO, 2,000 km), High orbit (GEO, 8,140 km: one lap takes exactly one Terra day), Luna orbit or Luna landing. Click one, or press 1–5 (0 = free flight).Tap one. You can change your mind, or go back to free flight, any time before liftoff. In flight, a pink panel tells you the next step in a few words (“Tilt left to 45°”, “Cut engine now”, “Wait — warp to Ap”, “Burn prograde at Ap”), with a throttle cue (FULL / CUT / a %). A pink arrow next to the rocket and a pink marker on the dial show where to point, and a curved arrow shows which way to turn. The Angle reading helps: 90° is straight up, 0° is flat along the horizon (near Luna it's Luna's horizon). When you're told to warp, time warp slows down by itself before the next burn. Do what the panel says and you'll get there: reach the orbit (or land) and a MISSION COMPLETE banner appears. It's saved, and the Tracking Station shows your stars.

Launch pads

Terra has three launch pads. Pick one in the Launch pad row of the mission panel while the clock holds at T-10 (clicktap it) and your rocket moves there. Left to right: Cape Halcyon (on the coast, about 390 km to the left: fly left and you go out over the sea), Prime Field (the original pad, in the middle) and Redrock Range (in the desert, about 210 km to the right). The pad you pick is remembered for your next launches. Everything else works the same from every pad, and the map shows all three. Tip: the flight numbers panel starts short; tap it to see everything (and again to shorten it).

The edge of Terra's pull

Terra's pull reaches out 84,000 km (the dashed yellow circle on the map). Go faster than escape speed and your Apoapsis says Escaping; the map marks where you'll leave. Past the edge you're in deep space: nothing pulls on you, so you coast in a straight line and the distance keeps counting up (in km, then million km). To come back, turn round and burn towards Terra; once you're back inside the circle Terra's pull takes over again. In the map, an arrow at the screen edge points to a ship that's far away (CCentre zooms out to it).

Stars and personal bests

After MISSION COMPLETE a results screen shows your time (liftoff to done), the fuel left in the upper stage and the fuel used (and your touchdown speed for a Luna landing), with 1 to 3 stars: ★ for finishing, +★ for saving fuel, +★ for a neat finish.
Low orbitfuel: at least 60% left in the upper stage · neat: highest and lowest point no more than 15 km apart
Medium orbitfuel: at least 33% · neat: no more than 300 km apart
High orbitfuel: at least 28% · neat: no more than 1,221 km apart
Luna orbitfuel: at least 30% · neat: lowest and highest point both between 50 and 200 km
Luna landingfuel: at least 10% · neat: touch down at 3 m/s or slower
Your best stars, fastest time, least fuel used and softest touchdown for each mission are saved and shown in the Tracking Station.

Sound, music and shake

Every sound is made by the game itself. Each engine sounds different, the roar fades as the air thins (in space you only hear the rumble through the ship), and there are sounds for the countdown, liftoff, staging, the fairing, the parachute, touchdown, explosions, the max-Q alarm and Mission Complete, plus soft space music. Menu → Settings: master, effects and music volume, sound on/off, and camera shake on/off. Saved on this device.

What you'll see

Terra has clouds, beaches and shallow water along its coasts, and days and nights (the sun goes round once per Terra day; watch for the orange glow at sunrise and sunset). Luna has hills and craters: the ground you see is the ground you land on. Engines kick up dust on Luna and smoke on Terra near the ground.
